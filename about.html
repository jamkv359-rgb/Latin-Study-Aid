What is Latin Study Aid?

Latin Study Aid (LSA) is a free, non-commercial interactive platform for learning and revising Latin.

LSA combines vocabulary, grammar, translation, games, flashcards, progress tracking and personalised revision tools within a single learning environment. Rather than treating Latin vocabulary and grammar as static lists or tables, LSA uses structured data to generate interactive learning activities dynamically.

The project was originally created as a personal programming project and has developed into a broader educational resource with support from the [School Name] Latin Department.

LSA is designed to complement existing Latin courses and resources rather than replace them.

The idea behind LSA

Latin has an enormous amount of existing educational material, but the experience of studying it can involve moving between vocabulary lists, textbooks, grammar tables, worksheets and separate revision tools.

LSA explores a different approach: the data contains the information; the learning engine turns that information into an interactive experience.

A structured vocabulary entry can therefore be used to generate a flashcard, vocabulary test, game, search result, progress record or personalised revision recommendation without each activity requiring its own separate database.

This architecture also allows new resources and learning activities to be added without rebuilding the entire platform.

Project Status

LSA is an independently developed educational project and is not an official product of any textbook publisher, examination board, university or other external organisation unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The current public demonstration version focuses on independently reusable, openly licensed and original material. Additional curriculum-specific resources are being investigated separately, and material requiring permission is not included in the public demonstration dataset unless appropriate rights have been established.

Material Requiring Permission

Some Latin educational resources are associated with commercial textbooks, courses or other copyrighted publications.

LSA does not assume that educational or non-commercial use automatically grants permission to reproduce such material publicly.

Accordingly, material for which public redistribution rights have not been established is kept separate from the public demonstration dataset while permission or other appropriate rights are investigated.

This particularly applies to course-specific material where the underlying vocabulary lists, exercises, practice sentences or translations may be associated with copyrighted educational resources.
